# Type-II outer mean-square: one-sided Cauchy / double-large-sieve barrier — 2026-09-17

## 1. Target
On dyadic factor blocks

\[
n\asymp Q,\qquad a\asymp M,\qquad b\asymp L,\qquad ML\asymp D,
\]

consider

\[
T_n=
\sum_{a\asymp M}\sum_{b\asymp L}
\alpha_a\beta_b
 e\!\left(2K\sqrt n\,a^{-1/2}b^{-1/2}\right),
\]

where the Vaughan Type-II coefficients are divisor-bounded and can be absorbed into `D^epsilon` losses.

The required unweighted mean-square target is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E(Q;M,L)
:=\sum_{n\asymp Q}|T_n|^2
\ll_\varepsilon QD^{1+\varepsilon}.
}
\]

The reciprocal resolution and normalized oscillation are

\[
H=\frac{D^{3/2}}{K\sqrt Q},
\qquad
F\asymp\frac DH,
\qquad
1\lesssim H\lesssim D^{1/2}.
\]

Type II has both factors at least at the decomposition scale,

\[
M,L\gtrsim H^{1/2}.
\]

## 2. One-sided Cauchy
Apply Cauchy in the `a` variable:

\[
\mathcal E
\le
M\sum_{n\asymp Q}\sum_{a\asymp M}
\left|
\sum_{b\asymp L}\beta_b
 e\!\left(FU_{n,a}Y_b\right)
\right|^2,
\]

with normalized variables

\[
U_{n,a}=\left(\frac nQ\right)^{1/2}
\left(\frac aM\right)^{-1/2},
\qquad
Y_b=\left(\frac bL\right)^{-1/2}.
\]

After expanding the square, the phase is

\[
F U_{n,a}(Y_b-Y_{b'}).
\]

Thus the problem has exactly the two point sets needed for the Bombieri–Iwaniec double large sieve:

- `U`-points indexed by `(n,a)`;
- difference points `V_{b,b'}=Y_b-Y_{b'}`.

## 3. Optimal spacing input
For the two-variable monomial point set `U`, the standard Fouvry–Iwaniec/Robert–Sargos spacing estimate gives, up to logarithms,

\[
\mathcal B_U
\ll
QM+\frac{Q^2M^2}{F}.
\]

For the difference set generated by `Y_b=(b/L)^{-1/2}`, Robert–Sargos' four-variable spacing theorem applies because the exponent `-1/2` is nonzero and different from `1`. It gives

\[
\mathcal B_V
\ll_\varepsilon
L^{2+\varepsilon}+\frac{L^{4+\varepsilon}}F.
\]

The double large sieve therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E
\ll_\varepsilon
M F^{1/2}
\left(QM+\frac{Q^2M^2}{F}\right)^{1/2}
\left(L^2+\frac{L^4}{F}\right)^{1/2}
D^\varepsilon.
}
\]

This already uses the sharp Robert–Sargos spacing scale. The issue is not a crude spacing lemma.

## 4. Exact deficit against the required scale
Since `D=ML`, divide by the target `QD=QML`. The method produces the normalized factor

\[
R_M=
\sqrt{\frac{FM}{Q}}
\sqrt{1+\frac{QM}{F}}
\sqrt{1+\frac{L^2}{F}}.
\]

Hence

\[
R_M^2
=
\left(M^2+\frac{FM}{Q}\right)
\left(1+\frac{L^2}{F}\right)
\ge M^2.
\]

Equivalently, if the product is expanded before normalization, one of the unavoidable terms in this standard upper bound is

\[
M F^{1/2}
\left(\frac{QM}{F^{1/2}}\right)L
=QM^2L
=(QD)M.
\]

Therefore the one-sided Cauchy / double-large-sieve architecture misses the desired mean-square scale by at least a factor `M` when Cauchy is applied in the `a` variable.

Interchanging `a` and `b` gives the symmetric lower method-loss factor `L`. Optimizing the choice of Cauchy variable leaves

\[
\boxed{
\text{method deficit}\ \ge\ \min(M,L).
}
\]

Because Vaughan Type II has

\[
\min(M,L)\gtrsim H^{1/2},
\]

the direct method has at least a growing `H^{1/2}` deficit throughout the genuinely unresolved sector. At the extreme `H\asymp D^{1/2}` this is as large as `D^{1/4}`.

Classification:

`TYPEII_ONE_SIDED_CAUCHY_DLS_BARRIER`.

## 5. What this does and does not prove
This is a barrier for the **specified proof architecture**, not a lower bound for the actual Type-II mean square.

It shows that the following sequence cannot by itself close the target uniformly:

1. Cauchy in one Type-II factor;
2. discard coefficient signs except for divisor bounds;
3. count the `(n,a)` monomial points at resolution `1/F`;
4. use even optimal Robert–Sargos four-variable spacing for the other factor;
5. apply the standard double large sieve.

The dense term `(QM)^2/F` in the first spacing count, combined with the outer Cauchy factor `M`, already creates the `M` deficit. Improving only the four-variable difference spacing cannot remove it.

## 6. Rectangular factorization audit
The exact identity

\[
(ab)^{-1/2}-(a'b)^{-1/2}-(ab')^{-1/2}+(a'b')^{-1/2}
=
(a^{-1/2}-a'^{-1/2})(b^{-1/2}-b'^{-1/2})
\]

remains important, but in the one-sided Cauchy route above it only improves the **difference-spacing side**. The fatal loss comes from the dense `(n,a)` spacing term before that improvement can help.

Therefore:

`RECTANGULAR_FACTORIZATION_NOT_ENOUGH_AFTER_ONE_SIDED_CAUCHY`.

A viable use of the factorization must couple both factor differences before paying the full one-sided Cauchy loss, or exploit the arithmetic Type-II coefficient signs rather than replacing them by arbitrary bounded coefficients.

## 7. Literature correction
Robert and Sargos (2006), *Three-dimensional exponential sums with monomials*, treats

\[
\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{n\sim N}a(h,n)
\sum_{m\sim M}b(m)
 e\!\left(
X\frac{h^\beta n^\gamma m^\alpha}{H^\beta N^\gamma M^\alpha}
\right)
\]

for fixed real exponents satisfying `alpha(alpha-1) beta gamma != 0`, so the exponent pattern `(1/2,-1/2,-1/2)` is within its real monomial functional class after relabeling variables.

Its Theorem 1 is therefore directly relevant literature for the **linear trilinear sum**. However our required object is the sharper outer mean square

\[
\sum_{n\asymp Q}|T_n|^2,
\]

with specific Vaughan coefficient structure. Direct dualization of a linear trilinear bound loses too much, while the one-sided Cauchy/DLS adaptation above still carries the `min(M,L)` deficit.

Thus replace the earlier broad label `REAL_THREE_VARIABLE_MONOMIAL_LITERATURE_OPEN` by the narrower statement:

`EXACT_TYPEII_OUTER_MEAN_SQUARE_LITERATURE_MATCH_OPEN`.

Pliego (2024) gives further three-dimensional monomial estimates in a more specialized exponent/range geometry; it is relevant for comparison but does not immediately match the present sign pattern and mean-square target.

## 8. New live core
The next proof attempt must avoid at least one of the two losses that create the barrier:

1. **avoid one-sided Cauchy before coupling both factor differences**, e.g. a genuinely rectangular/two-sided differencing scheme; or
2. **retain arithmetic coefficient structure**, so that the dense `(n,a)` spacing sector receives signed cancellation rather than being counted absolutely.

The clean next audit is therefore a two-sided rectangular A-process / fourth-moment reduction. Its purpose is not to prove RH immediately, but to determine whether the exact product difference

\[
\Delta a^{-1/2}\,\Delta b^{-1/2}
\]

can reduce the `H^{1/2}` method deficit without importing a Chowla-strength assumption.

# Localized Selberg symmetry and the Type-II barrier

Status: exact decomposition / standard-method identification; no RH proof.

## 1. Localized semiprime channel
For a smooth weight W supported in a fixed compact subinterval of (0,infinity), define
\[
B_W(X)=\sum_{a,b\ge1}\Lambda(a)\Lambda(b)W(ab/X).
\]
This is the localized version of the semiprime term in Selberg's symmetry formula.

Choose a splitting parameter U. By symmetry,
\[
B_W=B_{\rm small}+B_{\rm bal},
\]
where B_small contains terms with min(a,b)<=U and B_bal contains terms with a,b>U.

For W supported, say, on [1,2] and U=X^{1/3}, every term in the balanced region satisfies
\[
X^{1/3}<a,b\ll X^{2/3}.
\]
Thus the difficult residual is exactly a balanced multiplicative bilinear box.

This is the same geometry isolated as a Type-II sum by Vaughan's identity: Type-I pieces have a genuinely small divisor variable; Type-II pieces have two medium-sized factor variables.

## 2. Absolute-value exponent inflation
Let
\[
\psi(y)=y+E(y),\qquad E(y)=O(y^\theta L(y))
\]
with a slowly varying logarithmic factor L. The unweighted convolution has the exact Dirichlet-hyperbola identity
\[
C(x):=\sum_{ab\le x}\Lambda(a)\Lambda(b)
=2\sum_{a\le\sqrt x}\Lambda(a)\psi(x/a)-\psi(\sqrt x)^2.
\]
Substituting psi=y+E and estimating the E-terms absolutely gives
\[
\sum_{a\le\sqrt x}\Lambda(a)|E(x/a)|
\ll x^\theta L(x)\sum_{a\le\sqrt x}\frac{\Lambda(a)}{a^\theta}
\ll x^{(1+\theta)/2}L(x)\log^A x.
\]
The boundary term \(\sqrt x E(\sqrt x)\) has the same power. Hence
\[
\boxed{
C(x)-C_{\rm main}(x)
=O\left(x^{(1+\theta)/2}\log^A x\right)
}
\]
under absolute-value propagation of a theta-level prime error.

The exponent map is
\[
\boxed{\theta\mapsto(1+\theta)/2.}
\]
It moves every theta<1 toward 1. In particular theta=1/2 becomes 3/4 rather than improving.

Classification: `HYPERBOLA_EXPONENT_INFLATION`.

## 3. Why Type-II cancellation is unavoidable
The weighted prime term
\[
A(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)\log n
\]
has error of the same exponent theta, up to logarithms, by partial summation. Therefore in the Selberg identity the semiprime error generated by absolute hyperbola control is larger than the prime error whenever theta<1.

To retain a theta-level polynomial error in a localized symmetry formula one must exploit cancellation inside the balanced factor range. This is precisely the role of Type-II estimates in Vaughan/Heath-Brown decompositions.

Hence
\[
\boxed{
\text{localized Selberg symmetry + RH-scale control}
\Longrightarrow\text{genuine Type-II cancellation input.}
}
\]
No positivity or one-sided estimate for the original nonnegative prime/semiprime terms supplies this cancellation.

## 4. Centered formulation
Writing a centered prime measure schematically as
\[
r=\Lambda-\text{main density},
\]
the semiprime channel contains a quadratic residual r*r. Its balanced part is the phase-sensitive Type-II term. Cauchy--Schwarz replaces this signed bilinear information by mean-square information; in short intervals this leads into Selberg-integral / prime-variance estimates, which are themselves RH-level or zero-correlation level inputs at the relevant strength.

Thus a plain L2 treatment does not create a pointwise square-root bound for the prime error.

## 5. Fixed finite Selberg hierarchy is not a complete zero detector
For
\[
\Lambda_k=\mu*\log^k,
\qquad
\sum_n\Lambda_k(n)n^{-s}=(-1)^k\frac{\zeta^{(k)}(s)}{\zeta(s)},
\]
a simple zero rho is detected in a fixed k-channel through the local Taylor coefficient \(\zeta^{(k)}(\rho)\). No general principle forces each finite set of these higher coefficients to be quantitatively nonzero at every hypothetical zero.

Therefore no fixed finite collection \(k\le K\) is a uniformly complete zero detector on purely local analytic grounds. Taking all k reconstructs the full local germ and hence returns the original analytic information.

Classification: `FINITE_SELBERG_ORDER_DETECTOR_BARRIER`.

## 6. Project verdict
The localized Selberg route does preserve polynomial exponents at the formal spectral level, but the arithmetic estimate needed to exploit this is exactly the difficult balanced Type-II cancellation. The classical symmetry formula itself suppresses zero singularity and its elementary recursive inequality averages away polynomial exponents.

Therefore this branch is closed unless a new arithmetic mechanism supplies Type-II cancellation beyond Vaughan/Heath-Brown/large-sieve/short-interval mean-square methods.

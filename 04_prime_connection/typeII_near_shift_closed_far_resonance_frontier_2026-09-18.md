# Type-II near-shift closure / far-resonance frontier — 2026-09-18

## Status
- Riemann hypothesis: OPEN.
- Weight-adapted very-small-`Q` target: retained.
- New exact localization: after one Cauchy step, the Type-II shift range `h <= cH` is controlled at the natural `D H` scale by the first-derivative test, uniformly in the factor split.
- Therefore the reciprocal `K^(1/4)` deficit of the generic DLS architecture is **not generated by the unresolved near shift cell itself**.
- The remaining loss is concentrated in `h >= cH`, where the derivative crosses integer frequencies and generic spacing produces the invariant dense term.
- In the balanced `Q=1`, `D=K` box, the whole difference range is of size `H`, so the arbitrary-coefficient bilinear operator is already at the required `K^(3/4+eps)` amplitude up to logarithms.
- Unbalanced boxes still contain a far-resonant range and remain open when the long factor is the actual Möbius variable.

## 1. Bilinear Type-II model
For fixed outer `n~Q`, write

\[
T_n
=\sum_{a\asymp M}\alpha_a
 \sum_{b\asymp L}\beta_b
 e\!\left(2K\sqrt n\,a^{-1/2}b^{-1/2}\right),
\qquad ML=D,
\]

with divisor-bounded coefficients absorbed into `D^epsilon`.

The reciprocal resolution is

\[
\boxed{
H=\frac{D^{3/2}}{K\sqrt Q}.
}
\]

Apply Cauchy in the `a` variable:

\[
|T_n|^2
\ll M D^\varepsilon
\sum_{a\asymp M}
\left|
\sum_{b\asymp L}\beta_b e(\phi_n(a,b))
\right|^2,
\]

where

\[
\phi_n(a,b)=2K\sqrt n\,a^{-1/2}b^{-1/2}.
\]

After expansion in `b,b'`, let `h=|b-b'|`.

## 2. Shift-phase derivative is exactly governed by h/H
Put

\[
\Phi_{n,b,b'}(a)
=2K\sqrt n\,a^{-1/2}
\bigl(b^{-1/2}-b'^{-1/2}\bigr).
\]

For `a~M`, `b,b'~L`, the mean value theorem gives

\[
|b^{-1/2}-b'^{-1/2}|
\asymp hL^{-3/2}
\]

away from harmless dyadic endpoint constants. Hence

\[
|\Phi'(a)|
\asymp
K\sqrt Q\,M^{-3/2}L^{-3/2}h.
\]

Since `ML=D`,

\[
\boxed{
|\Phi'(a)|\asymp \frac hH.
}
\]

This identity is independent of how the Type-II product `D=ML` is split.

Classification:

`TYPEII_SHIFT_DERIVATIVE_H_OVER_RESOLUTION`.

## 3. Nonresonant near cell
Choose a fixed sufficiently small constant `c0>0`, depending only on the dyadic support conventions, so that for

\[
1\le h\le c_0H
\]

the derivative stays in an interval contained in `(-1/2,1/2)` and is monotone. Its distance to the nearest integer is therefore comparable to `h/H`.

The Kusmin--Landau first-derivative estimate gives

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{a\asymp M}e(\Phi_{n,b,b'}(a))
\right|
\ll
\min\!\left(M,\frac Hh\right).
}
\]

No Möbius cancellation is used here.

## 4. Aggregate near-shift quadratic form
There are `O(L)` pairs `(b,b')` for each fixed shift `h`. Therefore, after the outer Cauchy factor `M`, the absolute near-shift contribution is

\[
\ll
ML D^\varepsilon
\sum_{h\le c_0H}
\min\!\left(M,\frac Hh\right).
\]

Uniformly in `M`,

\[
\sum_{h\le c_0H}
\min\!\left(M,\frac Hh\right)
\ll H\log(2H).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
|T_n|^2_{\rm near}
\ll
D H\,D^\varepsilon.
}
\]

Classification:

`TYPEII_NEAR_SHIFT_DH_BOUND`.

This is exactly the scale produced by the weight-adapted small-`Q` criterion on the hardest `D~K` block:

\[
DH
=K^{3/2}Q^{-1/2}
\qquad(D=K).
\]

Summing over `n~Q` gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\asymp Q}|T_n|^2_{\rm near}
\ll
K^{3/2}Q^{1/2+\varepsilon},
}
\]

which matches the relaxed unweighted Type-II target.

## 5. Cauchy diagonal term
The `b=b'` term after Cauchy is

\[
\ll M^2L=MD
\]

for each fixed `n`.

If the Cauchy variable is chosen as the smaller factor, `M<=sqrt(D)`, then at `D=K`

\[
\frac{MD}{DH}
\le
\frac{\sqrt K}{H}
=\sqrt Q.
\]

Hence in the genuinely bounded or subpolynomial outer range

\[
Q=K^{o(1)},
\]

the Cauchy diagonal exceeds the weight-adapted target by at most `K^{o(1)}`, absorbable into the usual `K^epsilon` allowance.

This observation is limited to the very-small-`Q` edge and is not a replacement for the polynomial-`Q` mesoscopic band problem.

Classification:

`SUBPOLYNOMIAL_Q_CAUCHY_DIAGONAL_ABSORPTION`.

## 6. Balanced bounded-Q box
At

\[
D=K,
\qquad Q\asymp1,
\qquad M\asymp L\asymp K^{1/2},
\]

we have

\[
H\asymp K^{1/2}.
\]

Thus every difference `|b-b'|` lies in the near-scale range up to fixed dyadic constants. Splitting a finite number of endpoint/resonance subranges if needed, the preceding first-derivative estimate gives the operator scale

\[
|T_n|^2\ll K^{3/2+\varepsilon},
\]

or

\[
\boxed{|T_n|\ll K^{3/4+\varepsilon}.}
\]

This is precisely the weight-adapted endpoint requirement.

Classification:

`BALANCED_BOUNDED_Q_TYPEII_ENDPOINT_CLOSED`.

The claim is about the balanced dyadic Type-II model after standard endpoint partitioning; it is not yet a closure of all Vaughan orientations.

## 7. Where the generic DLS K^(1/4) deficit actually lives
For

\[
h\gtrsim H,
\]

the derivative `Phi'(a)` reaches and crosses nonzero integers. The simple first-derivative bound `H/h` is no longer valid globally. These crossings are the discrete reciprocal resonances which, after generic spacing/DLS treatment, generate the dense-spacing term.

Therefore the 2026-09-18 `WEIGHT_RELAXED_TYPEII_DLS_K_QUARTER_BARRIER` should be interpreted more precisely as a **far-resonance barrier**, not a failure of the unresolved near cell.

Permanent correction:

`TYPEII_K_QUARTER_DEFICIT_LOCALIZED_TO_FAR_RESONANCES`.

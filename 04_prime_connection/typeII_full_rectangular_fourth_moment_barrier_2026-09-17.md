# Type-II full rectangular fourth-moment barrier — 2026-09-17

## Status
- Riemann hypothesis: OPEN.
- Type-II outer mean square: OPEN.
- Full two-sided Cauchy coefficient removal: INSUFFICIENT.
- Exact rectangular phase factorization: VALID but overwhelmed by a diagonal fourth-moment loss if used only after full Cauchy.

## 1. Starting sum
For fixed outer `n`, write

\[
T_n=\sum_{a\asymp M}\sum_{b\asymp L}
\alpha_a\beta_b\,e(A_n x_a y_b),
\]

where

\[
x_a=a^{-1/2},\qquad y_b=b^{-1/2},\qquad A_n=2K\sqrt n,
\qquad ML\asymp D.
\]

The desired outer mean-square remains

\[
\sum_{n\asymp Q}|T_n|^2\ll_\varepsilon QD^{1+\varepsilon}.
\]

## 2. Full two-sided Cauchy
First Cauchy in the `a` variable gives

\[
|T_n|^2
\le
M\sum_{a}\left|\sum_b\beta_b e(A_nx_ay_b)\right|^2.
\]

Squaring and applying Cauchy to the full `(b,b')` pair set gives

\[
|T_n|^4
\le
M^2L^2
\sum_{b,b'}
\left|
\sum_a e\!\left(A_nx_a(y_b-y_{b'})\right)
\right|^2.
\]

Expanding the last square produces the exact rectangular phase

\[
A_n(x_a-x_{a'})(y_b-y_{b'}).
\]

Thus

\[
|T_n|^4
\le
M^2L^2
\sum_{a,a',b,b'}
 e\!\left(A_n(x_a-x_{a'})(y_b-y_{b'})\right).
\]

This is precisely the product-difference structure sought in the previous audit.

## 3. Exact diagonal floor of the obtained bound
However the quantity before the final expansion is a sum of nonnegative squares:

\[
\mathcal R_n:=
\sum_{b,b'}
\left|
\sum_a e\!\left(A_nx_a(y_b-y_{b'})\right)
\right|^2.
\]

For every `b=b'`, the inner phase vanishes identically and

\[
\left|\sum_a1\right|^2=M^2.
\]

There are `L` such pairs, hence exactly

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_n\ge LM^2.}
\]

Therefore the **upper-bound expression generated by this full-Cauchy architecture** has the unavoidable floor

\[
M^2L^2\mathcal R_n
\ge
M^4L^3.
\]

This is not a lower bound for the true `|T_n|^4`; it is a lower floor on the right-hand side produced by the proof method after all coefficient information has been discarded.

## 4. Consequence for the outer mean square
Using

\[
\left(\sum_{n\asymp Q}|T_n|^2\right)^2
\le
Q\sum_{n\asymp Q}|T_n|^4,
\]

a fourth-moment route would need roughly

\[
\sum_{n\asymp Q}|T_n|^4
\ll QD^2
=QM^2L^2
\]

(up to `D^epsilon`) to recover the target mean square.

But the full-Cauchy bound already carries the diagonal floor

\[
Q M^4L^3.
\]

Relative to the required fourth-moment scale this is

\[
\frac{QM^4L^3}{QM^2L^2}=M^2L.
\]

After taking the square root in the outer Cauchy step, the corresponding method loss on the mean square is at least

\[
\boxed{M\sqrt L.}
\]

If the roles of the factors are reversed, the symmetric loss is `L sqrt(M)`. Hence full two-sided coefficient removal is substantially worse than the one-sided Cauchy/DLS route.

Classification:

`TYPEII_FULL_RECTANGULAR_FOURTH_MOMENT_DIAGONAL_BARRIER`.

## 5. Interpretation
The rectangular identity

\[
(x_a-x_{a'})(y_b-y_{b'})
\]

is algebraically useful, but **obtaining it by full Cauchy on all factor pairs destroys too much information before the oscillation can be exploited**.

The fatal part is the exact zero-difference family `b=b'` (or symmetrically `a=a'`). No improvement in off-diagonal spacing can remove this term once the proof has been reduced to the nonnegative square sum above.

Therefore the next live possibility must use a **short A-process / truncated differencing range**, or preserve arithmetic coefficient cancellation on the diagonal family. Full rectangular Cauchy is closed.
